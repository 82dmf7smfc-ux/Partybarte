## Definition
Cutting the pad surface with a diamond disk to regenerate pores and asperities.
The disk rotates and sweeps between pad centre and pad edge
[sd-diamond-disc-conditioning].

## Physics
Reaction product and debris fill the pad pores and grooves during polish, which
glazes the surface and stops slurry spreading evenly. Rate falls as a result
[sd-diamond-disc-conditioning]. Conditioning removes the glazed layer and cuts
fresh asperities, which is what holds rate steady and extends pad life
[sd-diamond-disc-conditioning].

Conditioning runs in situ during polish, or ex situ between wafers
[sd-diamond-disc-conditioning].

## Typical values
Diamond grit shape matters for stability. Blocky grit gave the most stable
removal rate over conditioning times up to 20 h in one study
[springer-diamond-shape].
