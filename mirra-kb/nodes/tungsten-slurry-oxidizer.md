## Definition
The oxidizing component of a tungsten CMP slurry. It converts the tungsten
surface to an oxide film that the abrasive then removes [sd-w-cmp-oxidants].

## Physics
Kaufman and co-workers proposed the accepted mechanism. Tungsten is removed by
repeated passivation and abrasion, so chemical action to oxidise and mechanical
action to disrupt the passivating film are both required
[sd-w-cmp-oxidants].

The oxidizer choice changes the film that forms. Ferric nitrate produced a denser
oxide layer on tungsten while other compositions produced a porous one
[sd-w-cmp-oxidants]. Hydrogen peroxide is widely used in commercial tungsten
slurries for cost and oxidising power [sd-w-cmp-oxidants].

Oxidizer and catalyst together are strongly synergistic. Catalytic amounts of
ferric nitrate added to hydrogen peroxide raised tungsten rates by more than an
order of magnitude, to the order of 5000 A/min and above [sd-w-cmp-oxidants].

Inferred, no source: peroxide based slurries therefore have a pot life problem
that ferric nitrate slurries do not, since peroxide decomposes. Mix time is a
process variable.

## On the Mirra
Not established. Tungsten is listed as a Mirra application [entrepix-mirra], but

## Typical values
Tungsten rates above 5000 A/min are reported for oxidizer plus catalyst
chemistries [sd-w-cmp-oxidants]. Treat that as a chemistry capability and not as
a tool rate.
