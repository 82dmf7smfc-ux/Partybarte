## Definition
How fresh slurry reaches the gap between pad and wafer, how long it stays, and
how spent slurry and debris leave. The usual metric is mean residence time, the
average time for incoming slurry to replace what is already under the wafer
[sd-groove-width-mrt].

## Physics
Grooves are the transport path. Widening a concentric groove from 300 to 600 μm
raised both mean residence time and slurry utilisation efficiency
[sd-groove-width-mrt]. Higher polishing pressure lowered mean residence time
[sd-groove-width-mrt].

Rate follows supply. One study measured 1655, 1613 and 1551 A/min at 200, 150
and 100 cc/min [sd-groove-width-mrt]. Transport efficiency also depends on platen
speed and on the conditioning method [sd-groove-flow-numerical].

Inferred, no source: the flow rate effect above is modest, roughly 7 percent
across a doubling of flow. So a starved line shows up as a rate shift long
before it shows as an obvious defect signature.

## On the Mirra
Slurry arrives through a dispense arm onto the pad, so the delivered flow rate
and the arm position are both recipe items.

Inferred, no source: with three platens the tool has three independent supply
paths, and a partial blockage on one is a per platen rate loss rather than a tool
wide one.

## Typical values
Literature flow rates in the source above run 100 to 200 cc/min for a 200 mm
tool [sd-groove-width-mrt]. Treat that as a literature range and not as a
setting for this tool.
